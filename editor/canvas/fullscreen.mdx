Fullscreen review is the closest editor preview to the audience's experience. It removes surrounding workspace pressure and makes framing, readability, motion cadence, and visible artifacts easier to judge.

## Before entering fullscreen

1. Seek slightly before the section you want to review.
2. Deselect layers if you want a clean canvas before the transition.
3. Confirm preview mute and loop state.
4. Close any transient menu or text-edit state.
5. Start playback and enter fullscreen from the review controls.

## What fullscreen does not change

Fullscreen does not change composition dimensions, layer scale, crop, frame rate, timeline zoom, or render configuration. It changes only the presentation of the preview surface.

## Review passes

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Picture">
    Watch edges, safe margins, crop continuity, flashes, unintentional blank frames, and visual stacking.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Motion">
    Judge keyframed easing, title entrances, motion paths, and whether animated detail remains readable at speed. A live layout spring can appear after reflow, but it is not authored frame-timed output motion.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Captions">
    Check line breaks, time on screen, contrast, reading speed, and collisions with important picture content.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Audio">
    Listen for clicks, abrupt ambience changes, masking between speech and music, and overly long fades.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Tip>
  A fullscreen review catches perceptual problems; frame stepping catches boundary problems. Use both before rendering.
</Tip>

If preview stutters, reduce competing browser load and follow [performance diagnostics](/editor/troubleshooting/performance). A smooth H.264 render can still be produced from a composition whose live browser preview drops frames, but timing decisions should be confirmed carefully.
