Caption styling combines the selected subtitle style with ordinary typography, appearance, stroke, shadows, and layer effects.

## Typography

Choose font family and style, size, line height, letter spacing, alignment, and direction. Test the longest page in the Wayanad sequence. A style that looks elegant on two short words can fail on a place name and full phrase.

## Contrast layers

Use one or more of these deliberately:

- Text color with strong luminance separation.
- A subtitle style that renders a restrained background-like treatment, when available.
- Stroke for local edge contrast.
- One or two soft shadows for separation.
- A selected caption style that distinguishes the current token.

The caption inspector does not provide an independent background color, radius, or padding control. For a directly authorable caption box, create a solid or frame beneath the caption and time the two layers together.

## Box geometry

Place captions inside a consistent safe region and avoid lower thirds, faces, and important scene detail. The box width determines wrapping; its height must accommodate the configured maximum lines and line height.

## Style selection

The **Style** control reads the names exposed by the subtitle library and normalizes invalid or missing values to the default. Preview each candidate on actual transcript pages before choosing it.

## Accessibility targets

- Keep a stable location unless picture content requires a deliberate move.
- Avoid rapid page changes and single-frame words.
- Use sufficient contrast over both bright mist and dark foliage.
- Preserve exact spoken meaning.
- Do not rely on color alone to mark the active word.

<Tip>
  Build captions at full output size, then review at the smallest likely viewing size. Fine strokes and shadows can disappear when scaled down.
</Tip>
